Design intent

TERRACRAWL is a six-legged mechatronic reconnaissance concept combining the low-slung visual language of an arachnid with segmented industrial leg mechanisms. Its symmetric leg stations are intended to support controlled foothold placement, low-profile stalking, lateral traversal, and terrain assessment.

Structural language

A central armored equipment hull carries six paired mechanical leg modules. Separate upper and lower shells are treated as rigid structures, preserving panel seams, hard edges and UV-mapped materials. Articulation is expressed by object-level transforms, not elastic bending or skin distortion.

Proposed engineering architecture

Primary chassis: sealed lightweight metal-composite housing with internal cross-bracing and protected sensor/electronics deck.

Actuation: independent servo or geared brushless joint modules with encoders, torque sensing, and compliant control at ground contact.

Locomotion: alternating-tripod support, foot-lift recovery cycles, side-stepping, synchronized turns, low stance and staged obstacle response.

Perception: proposed stereo depth, inertial sensing, near-field ranging and foot-contact load assessment for terrain-adaptive gait selection.

Energy: interchangeable battery module and distributed motor drives with thermal monitoring; runtime and payload remain to be engineered.

Scale adaptations

Scout: compact close-range inspection and reconnaissance. Utility: reinforced cargo-capable inspection chassis. Heavy: larger remotely supervised rough-terrain demonstrator with upgraded actuators, bearings and structural sections.

Engineering validation

Version 2 adds a procedural contact-aware gait preview: stance-foot world locking, two-link IK target solving, slope-aware foot planning, support polygon checks, and simplified quasistatic load distribution. The chassis and mechanical shells remain visual proxies. It is not a rigid-body dynamics solver and is not physics certified; true torque, collision, motor limits and hardware behavior require parameter calibration and independent robotic testing.
