Concept / Engineering Asset Dossier

SC6T-HD — Modular arachnid recon platform

Six-legged industrial reconnaissance crawler with segmented manipulator tail, multi-zone luminous emitters, hard-surface exoskeletal assemblies and a compact sensor/crew compartment. Concept functionality includes inspection, telepresence, terrain survey and hazard detection; equipment is not rated as operational hardware.

Structural topology

Paired articulated front, middle and rear limbs terminate in independent ankle/foot joints. Each leg consists of hip, upper, lower and distal link chains. A central chassis connects a flexible spine, sensor head and ten-link posterior tail terminating in a stinger-like toolhead.

Rig and weight processing

The supplied 50-joint skin hierarchy and inverse bind matrix accessor remain intact. The 20 identified hard-shell/LED primitive groups have been converted to dominant-bone single-weight assignments to resist rubber deformation. Blended weights elsewhere—including the interior, cables, body and occupant geometry—are retained. Some individual metal pieces still span several adjacent rigid joint assignments; per-shell topology segmentation remains a production step.

Motion behaviors

Alternating-tripod locomotion, accelerated scuttle, retreat, strafing, directional pivoting, stalking, defensive rearing, leap recoil, probing, climbing posture, crouching, sensor sweep and tail strike. Each new clip is baked to glTF joint quaternion tracks at 24 samples per second; motion remains near the asset center.

Luminous system

Fourteen material groups—including leg LEDs, beam, warning blinker and instrumentation screen—contain emissive factors. The HTML studio independently pulses or strobes these material channels with speed, intensity, beam and reactor controls.

Engineering limitations

This release is not physics-certified. It preserves the original bind pose without per-joint manufacturing calibration. The baked motions are artistic joint trajectories, not verified collision-free, torque-feasible or planted-foot IK solutions. Ground contact, self-collision, finite joint limits, contact loads and robotic stability must be validated independently before use in hardware control.
